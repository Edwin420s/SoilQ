\section{Executive Summary}
\textbf{SoilQ} is an enterprise-grade hybrid quantum-classical machine learning and agronomic decision-support platform engineered to address real-world agricultural challenges in Sub-Saharan Africa. The platform focuses on smallholder maize yield prediction and soil fertility management across diverse agro-ecological zones in Kenya.

By integrating multi-season field trial data from Chuka and Kandara, SoilQ establishes a rigorous benchmarking testbed comparing classical tree-based ensembles and support vector machines against parameterized Variational Quantum Classifiers (VQC) and Quantum Kernel Support Vector Machines (QSVM). In addition to the core agronomic classification engine, the platform includes a complete reference suite of foundational quantum algorithm primitives, an interactive Streamlit advisory dashboard, a production-grade FastAPI microservice, and 66 automated unit and integration tests.

\section{Agronomic Problem Statement \& Dataset Provenance}
Smallholder maize farming in Kenya is severely constrained by acidic Humic Nitisols, erratic bimodal rainfall distribution, and declining soil organic carbon. Farmers must make time-sensitive management decisions (e.g., top-dressing, organic manure amendment, residue retention) early in the vegetative crop cycle when symptoms of nutrient deficiencies are difficult to differentiate visually.

We utilize an open-access field experiment dataset published by \textit{Mutuku et al.} (Mendeley Data v2, CC BY 4.0), comprising \textbf{448 plot-season observations} across two contrasting agro-ecological zones:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chuka, Tharaka-Nithi County} ($0^\circ 20'\text{S}, 37^\circ 38'\text{E}$; 1477 m ASL): Acidic Humic Nitisol, characterized by high aluminum saturation.
    \item \textbf{Kandara, Murang'a County} ($0^\circ 59'\text{S}, 37^\circ 02'\text{E}$; 1530 m ASL): Humic Nitisol, characterized by high clay content and high phosphate fixation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental Design \& Agronomic Treatments}
The field trial was structured as a split-plot Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD) across 4 consecutive cropping seasons: Long Rains 2016 (\texttt{LR16}), Short Rains 2016 (\texttt{SR16}), Long Rains 2017 (\texttt{LR17}), and Short Rains 2017 (\texttt{SR17}). The treatments comprise two tillage regimes (Zero Tillage \texttt{D0} vs. Conventional 15cm Tillage \texttt{D15}) combined with seven soil fertility input regimes (Control \texttt{C}, Inorganic Fertilizer \texttt{F}, Residue+Fertilizer \texttt{RF}, Residue+Fertilizer+Manure \texttt{RFM}, Residue+Manure+Legume \texttt{RML}, Tithonia+Manure \texttt{RTiM}, and Tithonia+Phosphate \texttt{RTiP}).

\subsection{Target Formulation \& Feature Selection}
To ensure balanced class distributions and avoid statistical artifacts, the binary target \texttt{high\_yield} is defined by an exact empirical median split:
\begin{equation}
y_i = \begin{cases} 
1 \quad (\text{High Yield}) & \text{if } \text{Yield}_i > 0.735 \text{ Mg/ha} \\ 
0 \quad (\text{Low Yield}) & \text{if } \text{Yield}_i \le 0.735 \text{ Mg/ha} 
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $0.735 \text{ Mg/ha}$ represents the exact sample median across all 448 observations, yielding exactly 224 positive and 224 negative observations. The feature vector $\vec{x}$ incorporates:
1. \texttt{Height\_6th}: Vegetative plant height at the 6th-leaf stage (cm)
2. \texttt{Height\_10th}: Vegetative plant height at the 10th-leaf stage (cm)
3. \texttt{SPAD\_6th}: Chlorophyll meter reading at 6th-leaf (nitrogen proxy)
4. \texttt{SPAD\_10th}: Chlorophyll meter reading at 10th-leaf
5. \texttt{Stover\_Mg/ha}: Above-ground stover biomass yield (Mg/ha)

\section{Quantum Machine Learning Architecture}

\subsection{Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC)}
The VQC model maps normalized agronomic metrics $\vec{x} \in [-1, 1]^d$ into quantum state space and evaluates expectation values of Pauli observables:

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{docs/figures/quantum_circuit.png}
\caption{Qiskit Variational Quantum Circuit: Continuous angle feature encoding layer ($RY(\pi x_i)$) followed by parameterized variational ansatz layer ($RY(\theta) \cdot RZ(\theta)$) with ring CNOT entanglement topology.}
\end{figure}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Quantum Feature Encoding}: Feature values $x_j \in [-1, 1]$ are encoded via single-qubit Pauli-Y rotations:
    \begin{equation}
    U_\Phi(\vec{x}) = \bigotimes_{j=0}^{d-1} RY(\pi x_j) = \bigotimes_{j=0}^{d-1} \begin{pmatrix} \cos(\frac{\pi x_j}{2}) & -\sin(\frac{\pi x_j}{2}) \\ \sin(\frac{\pi x_j}{2}) & \cos(\frac{\pi x_j}{2}) \end{pmatrix}
    \end{equation}
    The platform also supports a second-order ZZ-feature map for capturing pairwise non-linear feature interactions:
    \begin{equation}
    U_{ZZ}(\vec{x}) = \left(\bigotimes_{j=0}^{d-1} H\right) \left(\bigotimes_{j=0}^{d-1} RZ(2x_j)\right) \left(\prod_{j < k} CX_{j,k} RZ(2(\pi - x_j)(\pi - x_k)) CX_{j,k}\right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Parameterized Variational Ansatz}: For each variational layer (reps $L \in \{1, 2, 3\}$), parameterized single-qubit rotations $RY(\theta_{j,l,1}) \cdot RZ(\theta_{j,l,2})$ are applied, followed by a ring of two-qubit CNOT gates:
    \begin{equation}
    W(\vec{\theta}) = \prod_{l=1}^L \left( \left[ \prod_{j=0}^{d-1} CNOT_{j, (j+1)\bmod d} \right] \left[ \bigotimes_{j=0}^{d-1} RZ(\theta_{j,l,2}) RY(\theta_{j,l,1}) \right] \right)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Expectation Value \& Classification}: The Pauli-Z expectation value on the first qubit is computed:
    \begin{equation}
    \langle Z_0 \rangle = \langle 0 | U_\Phi^\dagger(\vec{x}) W^\dagger(\vec{\theta}) Z_0 W(\vec{\theta}) U_\Phi(\vec{x}) | 0 \rangle = \sum_{k \text{ even}} P(|k\rangle) - \sum_{k \text{ odd}} P(|k\rangle)
    \end{equation}
    This is mapped to class probability via a calibrated sigmoid transformation:
    \begin{equation}
    P(y=1|\vec{x}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-4 \langle Z_0 \rangle}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Optimization}: The parameters $\vec{\theta}$ are optimized using the COBYLA gradient-free optimizer minimizing regularized cross-entropy:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\vec{\theta}) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left[ y_i \ln P(y_i=1|\vec{x}_i) + (1 - y_i) \ln (1 - P(y_i=1|\vec{x}_i)) \right] + \lambda \|\vec{\theta}\|_2^2
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Quantum Kernel Classifier (QSVM)}
To overcome the non-convex optimization challenges and potential barren plateaus inherent to variational ansatz training, SoilQ implements a \textbf{Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM)}. The quantum circuit evaluates the exact inner product (state fidelity) in Hilbert space:
\begin{equation}
K(\vec{x}_i, \vec{x}_j) = |\langle \Phi(\vec{x}_i) | \Phi(\vec{x}_j) \rangle|^2 = \text{Tr}\left( |\Phi(\vec{x}_i)\rangle \langle \Phi(\vec{x}_i)| \cdot |\Phi(\vec{x}_j)\rangle \langle \Phi(\vec{x}_j)| \right)
\end{equation}
The resulting Gram matrix $K \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is symmetric, positive semi-definite, and satisfies Mercer's theorem. It is passed to a dual quadratic programming optimizer to locate the maximum-margin hyperplane, guaranteeing global convergence.

\subsection{Scientific Findings}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Classical Tree Superiority on Tabular Data}: Random Forest achieved the highest overall accuracy ($87.05\%$), reflecting the natural suitability of orthogonal decision trees for tabular vegetative metrics.
    \item \textbf{Quantum Kernel Advantage over VQC}: The Quantum Kernel (QSVM) achieved $73.22\%$ cross-validated accuracy and $0.7950$ ROC-AUC, outperforming the parameterized VQC ($66.07\%$) while training in under 0.5 seconds.
    \item \textbf{Non-Trivial Feature Learning}: Both quantum models learn non-trivial decision boundaries on real agronomic field data without mode collapse, confirming the feasibility of QML for agricultural decision support.
\end{itemize}
